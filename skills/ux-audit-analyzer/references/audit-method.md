# Audit method

Use these questions as prompts, not as a mechanical checklist. Report only findings supported by the supplied material.

## Navigation and information structure

- Can the user understand where they are and what belongs together?
- Are navigation levels and labels consistent?
- Can the user predict where an action or link leads?
- Is important information hidden under an unexpected category?
- Can the user return, cancel, or move between related objects without losing context?

## Scenario clarity and continuity

- Is the next action clear at every step?
- Does the interface preserve data and context between steps?
- Are prerequisites, consequences, and completion criteria visible?
- Does the user know what has happened after an action?
- Are repeated inputs or unnecessary detours introduced?

## Errors, prevention, recovery, and feedback

- Are errors prevented where possible?
- Are validation rules visible before submission?
- Do error messages explain what happened and how to fix it?
- Can the user undo, cancel, retry, or restore?
- Are loading, success, partial success, empty, and unavailable states represented?

## Tables, filters, sorting, and search

- Is it clear what is searchable, filterable, and sortable?
- Are applied filters visible and removable?
- Does the table preserve context during editing or navigation?
- Are actions discoverable and associated with the correct row or selection?
- Are empty, loading, no-results, error, and large-data states addressed?
- Is the density appropriate for scanning and comparison?

## Visual hierarchy

- Is the primary information or action visually dominant for the task?
- Do competing accents create false priorities?
- Are related elements grouped and separated consistently?
- Can the screen be scanned without reading every word?
- Does emphasis match business and user importance?

## Interface consistency

- Do the same elements behave the same way?
- Are terms, states, controls, and interaction patterns reused consistently?
- Does the interface mix conventions that imply different behaviors?
- Are differences intentional and understandable?

## Text, labels, and naming

- Do labels describe the result of an action?
- Are terms familiar to the target user and consistent with requirements?
- Are instructions placed where the decision is made?
- Are vague, internal, technical, or conflicting names used?
- Does important text explain consequences rather than repeat the heading?

## Accessibility

- Is meaning conveyed by more than color alone?
- Are text and important controls likely to have sufficient contrast?
- Are controls distinguishable, labeled, and large enough to operate?
- Is keyboard focus and navigation relevant and accounted for?
- Are errors, statuses, and dynamic changes communicated clearly?
- Avoid certifying compliance from screenshots alone. State what can and cannot be verified.

## Business logic and requirements

- Does the interface reflect supplied rules, statuses, permissions, and dependencies?
- Can the user make an invalid business decision because information is missing or ambiguous?
- Are pricing, eligibility, scope, ownership, and consequences explained at the right moment?
- Does stakeholder feedback expose a real requirement or merely a preferred solution?

## Missing states and edge cases

Check relevant states such as:

- empty;
- loading;
- no results;
- error;
- partial success;
- disabled or unavailable;
- first use;
- returning use;
- unsaved changes;
- permission denied;
- long content or large datasets;
- mobile or narrow viewport when in scope.
